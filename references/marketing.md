# Secondary SaaS Marketing Surfaces

Product UI is the skill's primary purpose. Use this playbook for a product-led
landing page, pricing page, launch page, docs home, or an in-product upgrade
surface.

## Start from the product truth

Use the real audience, problem, capability, proof, and next step. Protect legal
terms, factual claims, prices, and measurement hooks. Do not invent customer
quotes, usage numbers, logos, or guarantees.

## Compose, do not assemble a template

Choose one visual thesis that connects the product to the page. Build section
rhythm around the proof sequence. Avoid the default chain of gradient hero,
logo cloud, three equal feature cards, testimonials, and CTA unless each part is
earned by real content.

Show the product doing useful work. A product scene should clarify an input,
decision, transformation, or outcome rather than act as a decorative dashboard.

## Motion

Use Tier 1 motion for controls and Tier 2 for local product scenes. Tier 3 is
allowed for one rare explanatory or launch moment. It must be skippable,
responsive, readable without motion, and reduced to a stable composition when
`prefers-reduced-motion` is active.

## Responsive and conversion checks

- Keep the first promise and primary action visible without covering content.
- Preserve reading order and evidence on small screens.
- Do not hide important product detail only in hover.
- Test long headings, zoom, keyboard, reduced motion, slow media, and no-script
  fallbacks where practical.
- Treat pricing, login, signup, and upgrade actions as product workflows with
  complete pending, failure, and success behavior.
